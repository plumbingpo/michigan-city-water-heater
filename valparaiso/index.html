Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Valparaiso

Many premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.
