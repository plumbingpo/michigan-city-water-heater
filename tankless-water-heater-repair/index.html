The majority of tankless breakdowns announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see again and again in Michigan City, IN.

Michigan City Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Michigan City, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat shows up clearly in those numbers. Error codes narrow the search, but they rarely name the broken part outright. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

Once the cause is confirmed, most repairs are finished in a single visit. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Outlet temperature that surges and sags while water is running
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get
  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water
  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Most water heater failures start small: a popping sound from sediment, a damp spot under the drain valve, or a shower that goes lukewarm a few minutes sooner each week. If you notice any of these signs, contact Michigan City Water Heater at (219) 203-5179 for a prompt inspection.

Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair Matters

Tankless heaters look simple from the outside, but inside is a tightly controlled system. A single error code can come from four or five different faults, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric models bring a different hazard, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

Professional service is usually the cheaper path, too. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors under real flow, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
