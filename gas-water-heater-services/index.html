No hot water from a gas heater usually means one thing: the burner is not firing, and something told it not to. Gas heaters are built around safety. The control valve will not open unless a thermocouple or flame sensor proves there is a flame, and many models lock out if the flame arrestor screen at the bottom gets clogged or the heater senses spilled exhaust. So the real question is which safety did its job, and why. Here in Michigan City, IN, the answer is most often a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot or burner, a lint-choked air intake, a failing gas valve, or a vent that is no longer drafting properly.

Michigan City Water Heater does not guess with gas appliances. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • A tank that pops and bangs while it heats, a classic sign of sediment
  • A yellow, lazy, or sooty burner flame instead of a crisp blue one
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver

The Benefits of Professional Gas Water Heater Services

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, largely because you cannot see the biggest hazards. A fitting that is almost tight can seep gas for days before anyone notices. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. And many repairs, including gas valve and heater replacement, require a permit and inspection in most areas. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

Our technicians approach gas heaters with instruments rather than assumptions. They measure thermocouple millivolts, read gas pressure at the valve, test draft at the hood, check for CO around the appliance, and leak-test every joint they touch. The result is a repair that targets the real fault and a home that is safer for it. We also give you an honest assessment of the heater itself. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Clean dust from around the base so the burner gets plenty of combustion air
  • Install carbon monoxide alarms on every level of the home and test them monthly
  • Give the heater breathing room and keep flammables well away from it
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Check that the vent connector is secure and rising toward the chimney or flue
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
